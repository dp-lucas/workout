## 2. THE KR METHOD — CORE PHILOSOPHY

### Definition

The KR Method is a high-performance fitness and longevity framework that integrates three pillars:

1. **Functional Strength** — Compound, hybrid resistance training
2. **High-Intensity Conditioning (HIIT)** — Explosive bodyweight and plyometric work
3. **Intentional Joint Mobility** — Deliberate movement work to restore and protect range of motion

The goal: **build pain-free bodies that trust and outlast.**

### Core Tenets

| Principle | Description |
|-----------|-------------|
| **Build A Body You Can Trust** | Your body should be reliable — strong, mobile, and pain-free. Trust comes from knowing your body can handle what life demands. |
| **Never Stop Building** | Continuous, lifelong process. There is no "finished" state — only compounding improvement. |
| **1% Philosophy** | Small, consistent progress compounds over time. Named after the belief that daily 1% improvements lead to extraordinary long-term transformation. |
| **Rebuild, Don't Replace** | "You don't need a new body. You need to rebuild the one you have." The body adapts to what you stop asking it to do — and adapts back when given the right work. |
| **Movement as Trust** | Stiffness and pain aren't aging — they're lost range, lost strength, lost trust in your own movement. Reclaiming movement restores confidence. |
| **Direct Correlation** | "There is a direct correlation between the work you put in and the outcome you receive." No shortcuts, no hacks. |

### The Four Pillars (Instagram Bio)

1. **Joint Health**
2. **Mobility**
3. **Longevity**
4. **Mindset**

### M.O.V.E.

Referenced in his Threads bio as a guiding concept. While not formally expanded publicly, it appears to be the philosophical operating system behind the KR Method — an imperative to prioritize movement as the foundation of health and identity.

## 3. TRAINING METHODOLOGY

### Programming Architecture

The KR Method follows a **hybrid training model** that blends:

- **Resistance training** (dumbbells, bodyweight)
- **Explosive plyometric HIIT** (minimal rest, high output)
- **Mobility & joint health work** (integrated, not bolted on)
- **Core work in every session** (non-negotiable)

### Workout Structure Patterns

Korey's programs consistently follow this general structure:

```
1. WARM-UP (dynamic, movement-based — e.g., push-ups + WGS, traveling lateral lunges)
2. CIRCUIT / SUPERSET BLOCKS (2-4 blocks, 2-3 rounds each)
   - Alternates between strength moves and explosive/bodyweight moves
   - Typically timed (30-60 sec) or rep-based (8-20 reps)
3. AMRAP FINISHER (As Many Reps As Possible in a set time window)
   - Usually 5-minute blocks
   - Compound movements chained together
4. CORE / MOBILITY COOLDOWN (hollow body holds, stretches)
```

### Programming Principles

| Principle | Details |
|-----------|---------|
| **Repetition for mastery** | Highly effective exercises repeat across phases for maximum return on effort |
| **Phase-based progression** | Training phases increase in intensity to deliver consistent progress |
| **Workout repetition** | Workouts repeat in the second week of each phase (with fresh coaching cues) |
| **Core in every session** | No session excludes core work — it's foundational |
| **Minimal equipment** | Most programs need only a pair of dumbbells and bodyweight |
| **Time-efficient** | Sessions range 20-45 minutes; never more than 25 minutes in some programs |
| **Home or gym** | All programs designed for either environment |

## 6. ASSESSMENT METHODOLOGY

Based on his work with Dr. Ashton, Korey's assessment protocol includes:

1. **Pistol squat test** — Tests unilateral leg strength and reveals asymmetry
   - Start at ground level (bench/chair assisted)
   - 3 reps per leg, 3 sets
   - Reveals strength imbalances between dominant and non-dominant sides
2. **Body dimension measurements** — Tape measure assessments
3. **Body composition analysis** — Detailed body composition scan
4. **Blood tests** — Lipid panel
5. **Heart rate zone testing** — Cardiovascular baseline

His assessment philosophy: "What someone looks like does not necessarily translate into their actual health/fitness/strength."

## 8. RECOVERY & LIFESTYLE PRINCIPLES

### Recovery Philosophy

- Recovery is an **active process**, not passive downtime
- Prefers "implicit rest" — varying training demands daily so muscles recover naturally while maintaining daily movement
- Does not necessarily advocate scheduled rest days for advanced athletes (movement every day for mental health)
- For beginners and those recovering from injury: scheduled rest days recommended

### Sleep Protocol

- Aims for **9 hours 15 minutes in bed** per night
- Targets **~8.5 hours of actual sleep**
- Maintains a wind-down routine before bed
- Considers sleep a non-negotiable for training adaptation

### Nutrition Approach

- Goal-specific nutrition plans:
  - **Lose Fat Nutrition Guide**
  - **Healthy, Fit & Toned Guide** (for toning, cardio endurance, everyday mobility)
  - **Build Muscle Guide** (for strength and mass goals)
- "Recovery dinners" that work as hard as you do
- Rewiring the palate toward whole, nutrient-dense foods
- "Cheat meals" reframed as **planned indulgences** (no guilt)
- No fad diets — sustainable, evidence-based approach
