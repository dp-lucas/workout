# Minimalist EDT Training Program
### Escalating Density Training • Full-Body • 3 Days/Week

---

## Program Philosophy

This is a minimalist Escalating Density Training (EDT) plan adapted from Charles Staley's original framework. It is designed for someone working a 9-to-5 job who wants to stay strong, lean, and functional without bodybuilder-level time commitment.

Core principles:

- **Frequency.** Three workouts per week in ABA / BAB alternation. Across two weeks you complete six sessions: three A days and three B days.
- **Density over intensity.** Each main pair is performed for a fixed 15-minute Personal Record Zone (PR Zone). The goal is to accumulate more total reps over time, not to grind to failure.
- **The 5-rep cap.** Per Staley's original method, use a load you could perform for ~10 reps but stop every set at 5 reps maximum. This preserves form and lets you sustain work across the full 15 minutes.
- **Antagonist pairing.** Squat is paired with pull variations (Day A); Romanian Deadlift is paired with push variations (Day B). These are non-topographically related pairings — upper body with lower body — which Staley explicitly sanctions for full-body and fat loss programs to maximize total muscle recruitment per session. Pairing RDL with a pull movement would double-tax the posterior chain, so push is used instead. Practically, both pairings are designed around commercial gym setups: squat rack and pull-up bar share the same station; deadlift platform and dip bars are adjacent. No equipment transitions mid-block.
- **Movement coverage over the cycle.** Vertical pull, horizontal pull, vertical push, and horizontal push all rotate across A and B days, so over a month every plane is hit consistently.

## Movement Philosophy — KR Method Integration

This program layers principles from Korey Rowe's KR Method around the EDT engine. The KR Method is a high-performance fitness and longevity framework built on three pillars: functional strength, high-intensity conditioning, and intentional joint mobility — with the goal of building pain-free bodies that trust and outlast.

EDT already handles the strength pillar through density-driven progressive overload. The KR Method fills what EDT leaves on the table: structured mobility preparation, joint health maintenance, and daily movement practice on off days.

The two systems share the same DNA:

- **EDT's "athlete vs. exerciser"** maps to **KR's "1% compounding philosophy"** — both reject the idea that training is about suffering and instead frame every session as a measurable performance event. Small, consistent progress compounds.
- **EDT's "never train to failure"** maps to **KR's "rebuild, don't replace"** — both prioritize longevity over ego. Your body adapts to what you ask of it. The goal is to keep asking for decades.
- **EDT's "trust the density"** maps to **KR's "build a body you can trust"** — the PR Zone builds strength you can measure; the mobility work builds movement you can rely on.

The warm-up and cooldown are not fluff — they are what let you keep running the PR Zone pain-free for years. The off-day mobility is not optional recovery — it is the joint maintenance that prevents the stiffness, lost range, and lost trust in movement that Rowe identifies as the real cost of aging without intention.

## Load, Reps & Rest Rules

| Rule | Application |
|---|---|
| Load Selection | Choose a weight you could lift for ~10 reps to failure (10RM). You will never approach failure during a set. |
| Reps per Set | Start at 5 reps per set. Early in the block, 6–7 reps are fine if the weight feels light. Reduce to 4, 3, 2, 1 as fatigue accumulates. Stop a set early if form breaks before the rep target. |
| Rest Between Sets | Body-regulated, not clock-regulated. Use the three signals below to know the exact moment you're ready. Typically 15–25 sec early in the block, 30–45 sec mid-block, 45–90 sec late in the block — but follow the signals, not the clock. |
| PR Zone Duration | Set a timer for 15 minutes. Alternate between A1 and A2 until the timer ends. |
| Progression Trigger | Track total reps in each 15-minute block. When you exceed your previous record by 20%+, increase the load by ~5% the following session and reset the rep count. |
| Form Trumps Reps | If technique breaks, end the set immediately. Resume after a longer rest. Density is meaningless if form is sloppy. |

## Day A — Squat + Pull

Lower-body squat focus paired with vertical/horizontal pulling. Mobility prep opens the session; calves, core, and a cooldown finish it. Total session time: ~34 minutes.

**Exercise Notes — Day A**

*Mobility Prep (7 min):*
- **Cat-Cow + Hip Opener:** From tabletop, flow between spinal flexion and extension. On each extension, open one hip out to the side. Alternating sides, 90 seconds total.
- **World's Greatest Stretch:** From high plank, drive one foot up next to the same-side hand. Rotate the elbow down toward the floor, then reach the arm to the sky. 45 seconds per side.
- **Deep Cossack Squats:** Wide stance, shift weight side to side into a deep lateral squat. Straight leg's toes point up. 60 seconds alternating.
- **Quadruped T-Spine Rotations:** From all fours, place one hand behind the head. Rotate that elbow down toward the opposite hand, then open up toward the ceiling. 30 seconds per side.
- **Scapular Pull-Up Activation:** Hang from the bar with straight arms. Depress and retract the shoulder blades without bending the elbows. 30 seconds.

*Main Block:*
- **Front Squat (slant board):** Heels on a slant board for depth and ankle-friendly knee tracking. Keep the elbows high and the bar secure on the shoulders; use a cross-arm rack or straps if the rack position fatigues before your legs. Move at whatever speed allows maximum safe rep accumulation — control the descent, no crashing into the hole, drive up with intent.
- **Pull-Ups:** Pull fast, lower under control. The 5-rep cap and no-failure rule handle form protection.
- **Ring Rows:** Pull fast to the chest, lower under control. Maintain a rigid plank throughout.

*Accessories:*
- **Standing Calf Raises:** Full range — complete stretch at the bottom, full contraction at the top.
- **Hanging Leg Raises:** Controlled movement. Momentum turns this into a hip-flexor exercise.
- **Hollow Body Rocks:** Lie flat, press lower back into floor, lift feet two inches and shoulder blades off the floor, extend arms behind head. Rock forward and backward while maintaining the hollow position. Aim for 60–90 seconds.

*Cooldown (3–4 min):*
- **Couch Stretch:** Rear foot on bench or wall behind you, front foot planted. Squeeze the glute on the rear leg, stay upright. Deep hip flexor and quad stretch. 45 seconds per side.
- **Cat-Cow:** Return to tabletop and flow through 45 seconds of slow spinal flexion and extension. Decompress.

## Day B — Romanian Deadlift + Push

Hip-hinge focus paired with vertical/horizontal pushing. Mobility prep opens the session; calves, core, and a cooldown finish it. Total session time: ~34 minutes.

| | | Table Top Bridge | 60 sec | — |
| | | Bow Reaches | 60 sec | — |
| | | Push-Up + Downward Dog | 30 sec | — |

| | A2 | Push Variation — alternate each B day:<br>Day 1: Dips (vertical)<br>Day 2: Push-Ups (horizontal) | Cap at 5 | (paired with A1) |
| C — Calves | C1 | Seated Calf Raises | 8–12 | 2 sets |
| D — Core | D1 | Ab Wheel Rollouts | 6–10 | 2 sets |
| | D2 | Starfish | 30 sec | 1 set |
| M2 — Cooldown | | Dynamic Pigeon | 45 sec / side | — |
| | | Table Top Bridge | 45 sec | — |

**Exercise Notes — Day B**

*Mobility Prep (7 min):*
- **90/90 Hip Switches + Rotation:** Sit with both legs bent at 90 degrees. Windshield-wiper the knees side to side, pausing in each 90/90 position. Add a torso rotation toward the front shin. 90 seconds total.
- **Dynamic Pigeon:** From high plank, lift one leg up and back, then drive the knee forward and place the bent leg between your hands with the outside of the foot on the floor. Lower your torso toward the floor as mobility allows. 45 seconds per side.
- **Table Top Bridge:** Sit on the floor, palms behind you fingers pointing out, feet flat. Drive hips up into full extension, squeezing abs and glutes, gaze straight ahead. Hold briefly at the top, lower with control. 60 seconds.
- **Bow Reaches:** From a side-lying position, reach the top arm over and behind you in an arc, following it with your eyes. Opens the chest, shoulders, and lats. 30 seconds per side.
- **Push-Up + Downward Dog:** Perform a push-up, then press back into downward dog, pressing heels toward the floor and chest toward thighs. Flow between the two. 30 seconds.

*Main Block:*
- **Romanian Deadlift:** Soft knees, bar drags the legs, hinge at the hips, neutral spine throughout. Stop the descent when you feel a strong hamstring stretch — usually mid-shin to just below the knee. Do NOT chase the floor. Move at a pace that allows clean reps throughout the block.
- **Dips:** Lower under control, drive up with intent. Stop at upper-arms parallel if shoulders are sensitive.
- **Push-Ups:** Maintain a rigid plank throughout. Lower under control, press up fast.

*Accessories:*
- **Seated Calf Raises:** Full range — stretch at the bottom is where the soleus responds most.
- **Ab Wheel Rollouts:** Controlled extension, core stays firing throughout. Shorten the range if you can't maintain the position.
- **Starfish:** Lie on your back, extend arms and legs into an X. Simultaneously lift one arm and the opposite leg to meet above your torso, lower with control, alternate sides. Keep lower back planted on the floor. 30 seconds.

*Cooldown (3–4 min):*
- **Dynamic Pigeon:** Repeat from the prep — this time held longer and deeper as a static stretch. 45 seconds per side.
- **Table Top Bridge:** Slow, controlled reps. Focus on opening the chest and decompressing the shoulders after pushing work. 45 seconds.

## Progression & Tracking

**How to track:**
After every session, write down two numbers for the 15-minute PR Zone: total reps of A1 and total reps of A2. Example for Day A: "Front squat 60 / Pull-Up 32". This is the only data point that matters for progression. If you ever switch A1 to a different squat variant, start a new baseline — rep totals and loads are not comparable across variations.

**When to add load:**
When you exceed your previous best total reps in a PR Zone by 20% or more, increase the load on that lift by approximately 5% the next session. If you exceed your baseline by 40% or more — most likely after a deload week or during the first few weeks of neural adaptation — increase by 10% instead. Reset your rep count and start building density again. This is the entire progression model — no percentages, no periodization charts.

**What to expect:**
Density gains come quickly in the first 2–3 weeks (rapid neural adaptation), then slow. By weeks 4–8 you'll typically be adding load every 2–3 sessions. After that, gains become steadier and slower — which is normal and exactly what "staying fit" looks like.

**Deload guidance:**
Every 6–8 weeks, take one week where you reduce the PR Zone to 10 minutes instead of 15. No load increase, no rep chasing. Just movement quality. This prevents accumulated fatigue from compromising the next training block.

## Safety & Execution Notes

- **Mobility prep is the warm-up.** The 7-minute KR Mobility Prep block replaces any generic warm-up. It is structured, day-specific, and non-negotiable. Do not skip it to "save time" — it is what keeps the PR Zone safe and your joints healthy across months and years of training.
- **Knowing when to transition between exercises.** For your upper/lower pairings, the limiting factor between sets is cardiovascular, not local muscle fatigue — your legs rest completely during pulling, and your upper body rests completely during squatting. Use three signals: (1) **Breathing test** — when you can take one full controlled breath through the nose and brace with it, you're ready. Still mouth-gasping, wait. (2) **Bracing test** — before stepping back under the squat bar or pulling the RDL, take your breath and brace. If the brace feels solid, go. If it feels weak or rushed, wait 10 more seconds. (3) **Quality check** — ask "can I do 5 clean reps right now?" Honest yes, go. Honest no, wait 10–15 seconds and ask again. These signals naturally produce transitions of ~15–25 sec early in the block, ~30–45 sec mid-block, and ~45–90 sec late in the block. The 15-minute clock provides all the pressure you need not to over-rest; the signals tell you the minimum safe point to move.
- **Romanian Deadlift form.** Soft knees, bar drags the legs, hinge at the hips, neutral spine throughout. Stop the descent when you feel a strong hamstring stretch — usually mid-shin to just below the knee. Do NOT chase the floor.
- **The 5-rep cap is a strong default, not an absolute ceiling.** Early in the block, when fatigue is low, sets of 6–7 are fine if the weight feels unusually light. Taper down to 5, then 4, 3, 2, 1 as the session progresses. The rule exists to prevent form breakdown from grinding — not to cap reps when you genuinely have them.
- **Pull-up regression.** If you can't yet do 5 strict pull-ups, use a band assist or do negatives (jump up, lower under control). Same logic for dips — bench dips or band-assisted are acceptable substitutes.
- **Recovery comes first.** Three sessions per week is the prescription. Adding a fourth session because you feel fresh is how minimalist plans become non-minimalist plans. If life is busy, drop to 2x/week — never more than 3x.
- **Sleep, food, walking.** None of this matters if you sleep 5 hours and eat poorly. The minimalist program assumes a baseline of 7+ hours sleep, adequate protein (~0.7–1 g/lb bodyweight), and daily walking. Without those, the program will still work — just slower.

## Off-Day Protocol — KR Movement Practice

Rest days are movement days, not couch days. The body adapts to what you stop asking it to do — stiffness and lost range are not aging, they are the cost of inaction. Off-day mobility is joint maintenance that compounds over time.

### 10-Minute Morning Mobility (Tue / Thu / Sat / optional Sun)

Perform once through. No equipment needed. Can be done immediately upon waking.

| # | Exercise | Duration |
|---|----------|----------|
| 1 | 90/90 Hip Switches + Rotation | 90 sec |
| 2 | Low Lunge + Openers | 2 min |
| 3 | Bow Reaches | 90 sec |
| 4 | Quadruped T-Spine Rotations | 90 sec |
| 5 | Alternating QL Rocks | 90 sec |
| 6 | Cat-Cow + Hip Opener | 2 min |

This opens the hips, spine, shoulders, and core so your body feels better moving into the day.

### Joint Health Protocols (as needed)

If a specific joint is giving you trouble — particularly knees — use targeted rehabilitation circuits on off days. These are therapeutic, not training. Example knee protocol for pain above the knee:

**Circuit 1 (3 rounds):** Assisted Heel Elevated Eccentric Squats 8–10 reps, Supported Reverse Nordics 6–8 reps, Wall Sits to Single-Leg Extensions 30–45 sec / 8–10 reps, Pelloquin Isometric Step Down 20–30 sec per side.

**Circuit 2 (2–3 rounds):** Low Squat Pulses 15–20 reps, Eccentric Pistol Squats 5–6 per side, Couch Stretch 45–60 sec per side.

These protocols are drawn from the KR Method's joint health library. Use them when needed, not as a daily obligation.
